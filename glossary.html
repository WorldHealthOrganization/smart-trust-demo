    The terms the DPI-H for Health workshop’s policy-maker track hands every table, in plain language. Where the Reference Architecture (DPI-H) glossary (smart-ra) defines the same or a close concept, the term is smart-ra’s and so is the definition, word for word; elsewhere the definition is the handout’s (the handout, PDF). Each term also links to the Digital Transformation Handbook for Primary Health Care glossary (dth-phc) where it has one.

    Definition from says whose words the definition is, and links to them: the handout (PDF) or the DPI-H RA glossary. Also defined in links the same concept in the DPI-H RA, DTH-PHC and SMART Trust glossaries. same as: both glossaries mean the same thing. close to: nearly the same. covers: this term covers several of theirs. related: it only touches theirs. Every term is a candidate until a person adopts it.

    Infrastructure and architecture

    Term Definition Definition from Sheet Also defined in
    Digital Public Infrastructure (DPI) A set of shared digital systems that should be secure and interoperable, and can be built on open standards and specifications to deliver and provide equitable access to public and/or private services at societal scale and are governed by applicable legal frameworks and enabling rules to drive development, inclusion, innovation, trust, and competition, while respecting human rights and fundamental freedoms. (G20/UNDP) DPI-H RA    
    Health Digital Public Infrastructure (DPI-H) Health Digital Public Infrastructure (DPI-H) refers to digital resources that are built as a shared means for fulfilling multiple health agency functions. DPI-H RA    
    Foundational Digital Public Infrastructure (DPI-F) Foundational Digital Public Infrastructure (DPI-F) is the digital public infrastructure, such as identity, payments and data exchange, upon which the health sector can build. DPI-H is distinct from DPI-F and builds on it. DPI-H RA    
    Reference Architecture (DPI-H) A technology-neutral blueprint that captures the common building blocks, relationships, and design principles of a technical environment. DPI-H RA 2  
    Point-of-Service Applications Point-of-service applications are end-user application components (IT systems or software tools) used by individuals directly engaged in healthcare—such as clinicians, community health workers, and patients—that enable interaction with the broader healthcare ecosystem. DPI-H RA    
    Application programming interface (API) A defined, documented way for one system to ask another for data. Handout   same as DPI-H RA: Application programming interface
    same as DTH-PHC: Application programming interface
    Client Registry The Client Registry is the national authoritative source of health-specific person identity. It is the foundational health-sector component that maintains a unique, verifiable identity record for every person who accesses or is eligible to access health services within a jurisdiction. DPI-H RA   close to DPI-H RA: Master patient index
    Facility, workforce and product registries Authoritative lists of health facilities, health workers, and products such as vaccines and their batches. Handout   covers DPI-H RA: Health facility registry
    covers DPI-H RA: Health workforce registry
    covers DPI-H RA: Product registry
    Lifelong Health Record The Lifelong Health Record (LHR) is one or more governed data repositories, whether physically consolidated or virtually federated, that collectively maintain a longitudinal, person-centred record of an individual’s key health information across care settings and over their lifetime. DPI-H RA   close to DPI-H RA: Shared health record
    Electronic immunization registry (EIR) Records each person’s doses. Often the first working part of a lifelong record. Handout   related DPI-H RA: Lifelong health record
    related DPI-H RA: Registry
    Personal health record (PHR) Health information the person can see and control, for example in a wallet or citizen app. Handout   same as DPI-H RA: Personal health record
    same as DTH-PHC: Personal health record
    Terminology Service The Terminology Service is a governed, publicly accessible service that manages, publishes, and provides access to the standardised terminologies, code systems, value sets, and concept maps used across the digital health ecosystem. DPI-H RA    
    Logical information model The agreed structure of the data: elements, types and rules, independent of any software. Handout   related DPI-H RA: Logical information model repository
    Computable Decision Support Engine The Computable Decision Support Engine is a governed, shared application component that delivers context-appropriate, evidence-based guidance at the point of clinical decision-making by applying computable clinical rules and knowledge artefacts to person-specific health data. DPI-H RA   related DTH-PHC: Decision support system
    related DTH-PHC: Decision support logic
    Health information exchange (HIE) The layer that routes data securely between systems. Handout   same as DPI-H RA: Health information exchange
    Health Management Information System (HMIS) The Health Management Information System is a governed, shared application component responsible for the systematic collection, aggregation, analysis, and dissemination of population-level and facility-level health data to support health system planning, performance monitoring, resource allocation, and policy decision-making. DPI-H RA   same as DTH-PHC: Health management information system
    Civil registration and vital statistics (CRVS) Birth registration can start a child’s immunization record. Handout   same as DPI-H RA: Civil registration and vital statistics
    Interoperability Interoperability is the standards-based capability of heterogeneous information systems to communicate, exchange data, and use the exchanged information to execute functions effectively. DPI-H RA   same as DTH-PHC: Interoperability

    Standards and specifications

    Term Definition Definition from Sheet Also defined in
    Standard vs specification A standard is a general rulebook (FHIR). A specification says exactly how to apply it to one use case (the ICVP IG). Handout   related DPI-H RA: Open standards
    related DTH-PHC: Interoperability standards
    SMART Guidelines Standards-based, Machine-readable, Adaptive, Requirements-based, Testable Guidelines are a comprehensive set of reusable digital health components (e.g., interoperability standards, code libraries, algorithms, technical and operational specifications) that transform the guideline adaptation and implementation process to preserve fidelity and accelerate uptake. SMART Guidelines provide a five-step pathway to advance the adoption of best clinical and data practices, even if a country is not yet fully digital. DPI-H RA   same as DTH-PHC: Smart guidelines
    SMART levels L1–L5 L1 narrative guideline · L2 DAK · L3 machine-readable FHIR IG · L4 reference software · L5 dynamic, data-driven use. Handout   related DPI-H RA: Smart guidelines
    Digital adaptation kit (DAK) Software-neutral L2 content: personas, workflows, core data dictionary, decision logic, indicators and requirements. Handout 1, 3 same as DPI-H RA: Digital adaptation kit
    same as DTH-PHC: Digital adaptation kits
    Core data set The elements a country agrees to record for a use case, each mapped to standard codes. Also called a minimum data set. Handout 3 related DPI-H RA: National health data dictionary
    related DTH-PHC: Data dictionary
    Fast Healthcare Interoperability Resources (FHIR) FHIR is a standard for healthcare data exchange, published by HL7® DPI-H RA   same as DTH-PHC: Fast healthcare interoperability resources
    Implementation guide (IG) A FHIR specification for one use case: profiles, codes and tests. It can be adopted rather than authored. Handout   same as DPI-H RA: Implementation guide
    Profile A FHIR resource tailored to a use case, for example by making some fields required. Handout    
    International Patient Summary (IPS) A standard minimal health summary, including immunizations, readable across borders. Handout   same as DPI-H RA: International patient summary
    International Certificate of Vaccination or Prophylaxis (ICVP) The “yellow card”. The digital ICVP is its signed, verifiable form. Handout   same as DPI-H RA: International certificate of vaccination or prophylaxis
    International Health Regulations (IHR) International Health Regulations (2005), amended in 2024. The legal basis for the ICVP and other cross-border health documents. Handout   same as DPI-H RA: International health regulations
    SNOMED CT Systematized Nomenclature of Medicine Clinical Terms is a structured clinical terminology standard for use in electronic health records. DPI-H RA 3 same as DTH-PHC: Snomed ct
    International Classification of Diseases (ICD) WHO’s classification; ICD-11 is the current version. Handout   same as DTH-PHC: International classification of diseases
    covers DPI-H RA: International classification of diseases 11th revision
    covers DTH-PHC: International classification of diseases 11
    Logical Observation Identifiers Names and Codes (LOINC) LOINC is a common language (a set of identifiers, names, and codes) for identifying health measurements, observations, and documents. DPI-H RA   same as DTH-PHC: Logical observation identifiers names and codes
    openEHR An open standard for clinical data models (archetypes), often used to store records alongside FHIR for exchange. Handout    
    Conformance Proof that a system implements a specification correctly, usually by passing a test suite. Handout    

    Trust, credentials and wallets

    Term Definition Definition from Sheet Also defined in
    Verifiable credential (VC) A digitally signed statement, such as a vaccination record, that anyone can check is genuine and unaltered. Handout   close to SMART Trust: Verifiable Digital Health Certificate
    Issuer · holder · verifier The three roles: who signs the credential, who keeps it, and who checks it. Handout 4 covers SMART Trust: Issuer
    covers SMART Trust: Holder
    covers SMART Trust: Receiver
    Digital signature A cryptographic seal made with the issuer’s private key. Verifiers check it with the matching public key. Handout   related SMART Trust: Document Signer
    Public key infrastructure (PKI) The keys, certificates and rules that make digital signatures trustworthy. Handout   same as DPI-H RA: Public key infrastructure
    same as SMART Trust: Public Key Infrastructure (PKI)
    Trust anchor The national authority whose key vouches for the health sector’s signers. Handout 4 related SMART Trust: Trust Network Gateway – Trust Anchor
    Trust list A published list of keys accepted as valid issuers. The GDHCN shares these between countries. Handout   close to SMART Trust: Trust Lists
    Global Digital Health Certification Network (GDHCN) WHO Global Digital Health Certification Network. A trust network so countries can verify each other’s health documents. Countries onboard per use case. Handout   same as DPI-H RA: Global digital health certification network
    same as SMART Trust: GDHCN Trust Network
    Digital wallet An app where a person holds their credentials. A signed paper QR code can play the same role. Handout   related SMART Trust: Holder
    Digital Documentation of COVID-19 Certificates (DDCC) WHO’s earlier specification; much of its approach carries into the digital ICVP. Handout    
    Health certificate identifier (HCID) The unique ID of an issued certificate. Handout 3  
    Consent A person’s permission for their data to be used or shared. Rules for minors and dependants differ by country. Handout    

    Testing and convergence

    Term Definition Definition from Sheet Also defined in
    Sandbox A shared test environment with sample data where systems can try exchanges safely. Handout    
    Test suite Automated checks of a system against a specification. ITB, the Interoperability Test Bed, is an open tool used for this. Handout    
    Interoperability lab A standing national or regional facility for conformance testing and building local skills. Handout    
    Connectathon An event where teams test real systems against a specification together. It exposes gaps fast and builds skills. Handout 5  
    Convergence workshop AeHIN’s method for aligning a ministry and its partners behind one national architecture and investment plan. Handout    
    OpenFN An open-source integration tool used to connect legacy systems and migrate them to standards. Handout    

    Governance, planning and financing

    Term Definition Definition from Sheet Also defined in
    Digital health strategy The national vision and priorities for digital health. Handout    
    Enterprise Architecture The organising logic for business processes and IT infrastructure reflecting the integration and standardisation requirements of the organisation’s operating model. DPI-H RA   close to DTH-PHC: Enterprise architecture
    Costed investment plan A prioritised, costed roadmap from today’s systems to the blueprint. Handout    
    Digital Implementation Investment Guide (DIIG) WHO Digital Implementation Investment Guide. Steps to plan, cost and integrate digital health investments. Handout   same as DPI-H RA: Digital implementation investment guide
    same as DTH-PHC: Digital implementation investment guide
    Technical working group (TWG) Coordinates standards and architecture decisions across government and partners. Handout   same as DPI-H RA: Technical working group
    Decision rights Who has authority over architecture, standards, the core data set and the trust anchor. Handout 4  
    Procurement conditions Requirements written into tenders, such as conformance to the national IG, that steer what suppliers build. Handout    
    Total Cost of Ownership (TCO) The resources required to support a digital health intervention throughout its life cycle. DPI-H RA 6  
    Vendor lock-in Dependence on one supplier because data formats or interfaces are closed. Handout
