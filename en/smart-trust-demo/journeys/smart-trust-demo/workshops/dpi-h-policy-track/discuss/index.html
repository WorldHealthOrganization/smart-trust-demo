    When you don’t have it yet: start from three patterns

    Every sheet ends with the same eight-minute gap analysis. For each missing capability, ask whether it blocks you now. If it does, pick a pattern and name the debt it creates.

    Start narrow

    One population, one credential, one facility type.

    e.g. Hajj Health Card

    Start shared

    Build the foundation once for several programmes, not only for immunization.

    e.g. Registries across government

    Start borrowed

    Adopt a regional or international artefact instead of authoring your own.

    e.g. the ICVP FHIR IG, the IPS, or a SNOMED subset

    Table rules

    • Every table does the same task.

    • Each country grouping discusses, decides and documents it. If you cannot find agreement immediately, the disagreement is a finding: also write it down.

    Our Journey and Where It Breaks

    Write down how the immunization journey works in your country today. Then identify the digital capabilities that are needed for the immunization journey.

    DPI-H for Health WorkshopPolicy-maker track · Day 1 and Day 2

    1Our use case 8 min M1

    A child vaccinated in should be able to in .

    Discuss Each person says the use case in their own words. Agree one sentence the whole delegation can defend.

    2The journey 25 min · write, then circle one rating per touchpoint M1

    Discuss Each person tells your country’s version of one touchpoint before you write. Draw a ★ on the touchpoint where your journey breaks hardest.

    1 Vaccinated and recorded

    A child is vaccinated abroad, and leaves with a paper card and a QR code.

    Must be true: the dose is recorded against the right person, in codes that mean the same everywhere.

    2 Found and registered

    Back home, a community health worker finds an infant who is in no register.

    Must be true: the child can be identified and linked to records made elsewhere.

    3 A foreign record used for the next vaccine

    At the clinic, a nurse is shown a record issued in another country. Can she trust it? What is due now, given doses that were given somewhere else?

    Must be true: the issuer can be verified, and the data can be read here. the schedule logic reads every dose, wherever it was given.

    4 Continuity across border

    The family travels again, the record on a phone or paper QR, checked at a paediatrician office.

    Must be true: the person holds the record, and any verifier can check it.

    Every moment’s policy takeaway

    • J1
      A baby from Onesa is born in Taifafeki

      Babies born abroad can get a verifiable vaccine record before they have the host country’s civil registration or a national ID — and registering the birth at home still matters.

    • J2a
      A health worker finds the baby — and reads his cards

      Keep a clear line between what families report and what has been verified, and don’t grant trust too early.

    • J2b
      Registered at home, and referred

      Newborns can be registered at home, offline, before civil registration or a national ID exists.

    • J3a
      The clinic confirms the cards are genuine

      Trust is decentralized: a clinic in Onesa verifies a signature from Taifafeki itself, without contacting Taifafeki.

    • J3b
      Taifafeki vaccines are fitted to the Onesa schedule

      Never overwrite another country’s record: keep the original coding alongside the national one.

    • J4.0a
      One phone holds the whole family’s records

      Design for shared devices (“One Family, One Phone”) so families with only one phone aren’t left out.

    • J4.0b
      Dad creates a link to share at the clinic

      Data sovereignty: the record stays in Onesa until the family authorizes someone to open it.

    • J4.1
      A doctor in Taifafeki sees the full history

      Continuity of care across borders: each country keeps its own records, with no international patient registry and no vendor lock-in. What is agreed regionally is the data model and the trust list, not a shared copy of the data.
