    This page is part of the SMART Trust demonstrator: journeys J1 to J4 (v0.1.0: ci-build) based on FHIR (HL7® FHIR® Standard) v4.0.1. This is the continuous integration build: it follows the source and is not a published version. For a full list of available versions, see the Directory of published versions.

    Each module of the policy-maker track asks one question of the same story. This page shows where each module (M1 to M7) meets a step of the journeys (J1 to J4.1), and what is technical and what is policy at that meeting point. The same map is the Modules × journey step of the journeys viewer’s Discussion tab.

    ● = the module’s question is asked at this step in the walkthrough. ○ = the module draws on this step without asking there.

    Where they meet

    M1 · From journey to capabilities

    What had to exist for this moment to work — and where does our version of the journey break?

    Step   Technical Policy
    J1 ● Hospital immunization record; one DVC per birth dose, signed by Taifafeki’s document signer. The country writes its own use-case statement: which child, which record, which border.
    J2a ○ Offline CHW app reads the cards without verifying them. Where the domestic journey breaks first: a child no national system knows exists.
    J2b ○ Client registry holds a child with no birth record; referral to the clinic. Works / Partly / Breaks for a child born abroad.
    J3a ● Signature checked offline against the cached GDHCN trust list. What must be true for a nurse to trust a record she did not create.
    J3b ○ Product code mapping and the computable national schedule. Whether a dose given abroad counts at home.
    J4.0b ○ National portal issues a passcode-protected link to the IPS and DVCs. Whether the family, not the state, starts the sharing.
    J4.1 ○ Point-of-care viewer reads the whole first year from Onesa. Continuity across a border as the use case’s end point.

    M2 · The reference architecture and where you stand

    Which system do we use today for each block — and is it there, partly there, or absent?

    Step   Technical Policy
    J1 ○ Facility EMR, product registry, immunization registry, trust services. Which national block each of these maps to, and whether it exists.
    J2b ● Client registry, CRVS link, offline CHW app, facility registry for the catchment. Which block issues a child’s first identifier; how a birth abroad enters the registries and the coverage denominator.
    J3a ○ Clinic EHR as a verifier; trust store loaded from the national trust anchor. Whether verification sits in each clinic or in one national service.
    J4.0b ○ National FHIR server behind a gateway; IPS assembled from the lifelong record. Whether a national record exists for the link to point to.

    M3 · Standards, specifications and the core data set

    Which standards do we adopt rather than author — and who owns the national core data set?

    Step   Technical Policy
    J1 ○ Birth-dose data elements: vaccine, product code, batch, date, giver (immunization DAK core data dictionary). Who adopts the core data set rather than designing a national form.
    J2a ● A core data element that tells a reported dose from a verified one. Who defines that element in the national core data set.
    J3b ● Terminology service: Taifafeki’s product code kept, Onesa’s schedule code added beside it; schedule logic over every dose. Who owns the national core data set and cross-country code maps; whether vaccine code licensing is settled.
    J4.0b ○ IPS plus an immunization core as the part of the record that crosses borders. Adopt the IPS and the VHL specification; author only national extensions.

    M4 · Trust, credentials and wallets

    Who signs, who verifies, and who would hold the national trust anchor? Name it; do not decide it.

    Step   Technical Policy
    J1 ● Document signer certificate (DSC) and its key; HCERT/CWT signing of each DVC. Who signs on behalf of the health sector: hospital, ministry or a national trust service.
    J2a ● Read-unverified: the CHW device decodes but does not verify. Who is allowed to verify a foreign credential: a phone, a clinic, or only a national service.
    J3a ● GDHCN trust list (DID documents), cached for offline verification; kid lookup. Who holds the national trust anchor and accepts a foreign signature; what must be decided before onboarding to the GDHCN.
    J4.0a ● Family wallet on one shared phone, one profile and one VHL per member. Whether a wallet is a national service, a private app or both, and who approves apps that hold a record.
    J4.1 ○ Viewer verifies the VHL’s HCERT against Onesa’s DSC from the trust list. Who verifies abroad, and what a verifier sees.

    M5 · Governance, decision rights and the legal basis

    Who holds each decision — and what legal basis lets the record cross a border and be shared for a child?

    Step   Technical Policy
    J1 ● Credential issued to a child with no national ID. Legal basis for a facility to issue a health credential to a non-citizen child (decision right: trust anchor; legal basis: consent for dependants).
    J2b ● Temporary id created at a home visit, offline. Who may create a health identity for a newborn at a home visit, and on whose consent (decision right: architecture).
    J4.0a ● Parent holds the child’s record on a shared device. Who may hold and share a child’s record; the law on dependants.
    J4.0b ● Consent resource recorded; 4-digit passcode; 14-day expiry. Legal basis for opening a home record abroad on consent; procurement clause for IG conformance; who sets link protection.
    J4.1 ● AuditEvent logged in Onesa on every access, including failed passcodes. The agreement that every view abroad leaves an entry at home, and who reads it (legal basis: cross-border).

    M6 · Doing it together

    Would your neighbour need this too? If yes, why would you each build it?

    Step   Technical Policy
    J3a ○ One trust network for every programme that signs credentials. Build once for the region, not for immunization alone.
    J3b ● Code map between neighbours’ vaccine products. Whether neighbours build one mapping together instead of each building it.
    J4.0b ● Regional IPS + immunization profile with national extensions. Profile once for the region, keep national extensions sovereign.
    J4.1 ● Connectathon test of issuer, gateway and viewer. What the country sends to a regional connectathon within twelve months.

    M7 · First move and the investment required

    What is realistic in twelve months with the people and money we have — and who owns it?

    Step   Technical Policy
    J1 ○ Issuing to the WHO certificate specification. Candidate first move where issuance is missing.
    J3a ○ GDHCN onboarding and a clinic verifier. Candidate first move where verification is missing; recurrent cost of key management.
    J4.1 ● Whichever moment breaks hardest for the country. A twelve-month first move with a named owner, and the cost driver that dominates it.

    Technical overlaps

    Components that more than one module and more than one step depend on: build or test them once.

    Thread Modules Steps How it crosses
    Client registry and identifiers M2, M5 J1, J2b, J4.0a The same registry that gives Somsak a temporary id at home (J2b) is the one his IPS is assembled against (J4.0b). M2 asks whether it exists; M5 asks who may write to it.
    Signing and the trust list M4, M6, M7 J1, J3a, J4.1 One PKI carries three moments: Taifafeki signs the DVCs (J1), Onesa verifies them offline (J3a), and Onesa’s own key signs the VHL a Taifafeki viewer checks (J4.1). Built once, for every credential.
    Core data set and terminology M3, M6 J2a, J3b, J4.0b The reported/verified element (J2a), the product code map (J3b) and the cross-border IPS profile (J4.0b) are one core data set, read at three points.
    Lifelong record and the share link M2, M4, M5 J4.0a, J4.0b, J4.1 The national record, the wallet and the gateway’s audit log are one service seen from the family, the clinician and the ministry.

    Policy overlaps

    Decisions and legal bases that more than one module and step force: decide them once, with one holder.

    Thread Modules Steps How it crosses
    Trust anchor (decision right) M4, M5 J1, J3a, J4.1 Who signs at home and who accepts a foreign signature is one decision; M4 names the holder, M5 asks under what mandate.
    Consent for dependants (legal basis) M5, M4 J1, J2b, J4.0a, J4.0b The child acts through his parents from birth to the clinic abroad; the same rule decides who registers him, who holds his record and who may share it.
    Cross-border legal basis M5, M6 J4.0b, J4.1 Opening a home record abroad and logging the view at home need one agreement, which is cheaper to reach regionally than pair by pair.
    Core data set custody (decision right) M3, M6 J2a, J3b Whoever owns the core data set owns the code maps to neighbours; the room decides whether that is a country, a region or WHO.
